Questions and answers

Can an iPhone measure how accurate my mechanical watch is?

Yes. Timegrapher listens to the watch through the iPhone's own microphone and works like a watchmaker's timing machine. In about 30 seconds it shows the rate (seconds gained or lost per day), the amplitude (how far the balance wheel swings) and the beat error (the difference between tick and tock).

It works with automatic and hand-wound watches. The first watch is free, and there is no subscription. See it on the App Store.

How do I measure my watch with Timegrapher?

  1. Wind the watch and go somewhere quiet.
  2. Tap Add watch and enter the movement and its lift angle, if you know it.
  3. Tap Measure and rest the bottom edge of the iPhone firmly on the watch, ideally on the glass.
  4. Keep still. The measurement stops by itself at 0:30 and shows the rate, amplitude, beat error and a verdict.

Step-by-step guide.

What are good values for rate, amplitude and beat error?

These are the bands Timegrapher uses for its verdict:

  1. Rate: within ±5 seconds a day is chronometer territory. Up to ±15 is fine for daily wear. From ±30 the watch needs regulating.
  2. Amplitude: 250° to 315° is healthy for a wound watch lying dial up. Below 220° a service is due soon, and below 200° the oil has probably dried. Above 315° the balance risks knocking.
  3. Beat error: under 0.3 ms is well in beat. From 0.8 ms an adjustment is worth it, and above 1.5 ms the watch is noticeably out of beat.

Rates differ between positions, so note whether the watch was dial up, dial down or crown down. Measure in positions takes each one in turn.

How do I know if my watch is magnetized?

A magnetized hairspring is the most common reason a mechanical watch suddenly gains time, often minutes a day. Timegrapher checks with the iPhone's magnetometer: tap Check magnetism, measure the background field, then hold the watch against the back of the iPhone. The difference is the answer.

Take off any magnetic case, wallet or mount first. Every check is saved, so a check before and after demagnetizing shows whether it worked.

What lift angle should I use?

The lift angle is a property of the movement, and Timegrapher uses it to work out the amplitude. Many Swiss movements use 52°, but plenty use other values, so look up your calibre. A wrong lift angle is the usual reason a phone app shows the wrong amplitude. The rate and beat error do not depend on it.

How accurate is a phone timegrapher compared to a bench machine?

Timegrapher times each tick to a fraction of a millisecond and fits thousands of them against the iPhone's clock, the same principle a bench machine uses. A quiet room and firm contact give the best readings. Bluetooth headphones compress the sound, so disconnect them and let the iPhone's own microphone listen.

Treat the results as a guide. For a decision about a service, a bench machine and a watchmaker's judgement come first.

Can I check how my watch runs on the wrist over a whole day?

Yes. Rate from photos compares two photos of the dial taken a day apart. Each photo records the exact time it was taken, so the position of the seconds hand shows how much the watch gained or lost while you wore it.

Does it work with quartz watches?

No. A quartz watch keeps time with a crystal and moves its hand once a second, so there is no balance wheel to listen to. Timegrapher is for automatic and hand-wound mechanical watches.

Is Timegrapher free?

Your first watch is free for good, with every measurement. Timegrapher Pro is a one-time purchase that removes the limit on watches. There is no subscription, and purchases restore on any device with the same Apple Account.

Does Timegrapher record my audio?

No. The sound is analysed live and never recorded, saved or uploaded. Your collection stays on your iPhone. The optional price check searches Chrono24 for your watch's reference, and Identify from Photo sends that one photo to Apple's Private Cloud Compute only when you tap it. Privacy policy.

Privacy

Audio is never recorded.

Read the privacy policy

  • Microphone audio is analyzed live and is never recorded or saved.
  • Your collection and measurements are stored only on your iPhone.
  • The optional price check searches Chrono24 for your watch's reference.
  • Identify from Photo sends the photo to Apple's Private Cloud Compute only when you tap it. Apple doesn't keep it, and the developer never sees it.
  • The Clock tab gets the time from Apple's time server. The request carries no personal data.
