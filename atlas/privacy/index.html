Atlas

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026

Atlas ("the app") finds the countries you've been to in the photos on your iPhone. This policy explains, in plain language, what the app does and does not do with your information.

The short version

Atlas does not collect, store, transmit, or share any personal data. There are no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking. The app reads your photo library on your iPhone, and everything it works out stays on your iPhone. The app has no network code and makes no network connections.

Your photo library

With your permission, the app reads the date and location saved with each photo and video in your library, to work out which countries they were taken in. It matches each location against country borders built into the app, on your iPhone. You can give access to all your photos or only to the ones you select.

When you open a trip, the app shows that trip's photos from your library, at the size they are drawn. It never copies your pictures or changes your library. When you look at a photo kept only in iCloud ("Optimise iPhone Storage"), Photos downloads it from your own iCloud library, as it does in the Photos app.

You can change or turn off photo access at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Photos.

What the app saves

The app keeps four small files in its own storage on your iPhone:

  • the countries and visits it found;
  • your own changes (countries you added or removed, edited visit counts, trips you merged, and the photos you hid);
  • for each photo it counted, the photo's library ID, its date and its country, so a trip can show its photos (no coordinates and no image data);
  • your Photo sources settings.

It also remembers the list order, a few settings of the last scan, and how often the app has just been useful to you (a scan that found countries, a shared picture), which decides when it asks for a rating. None of this is sent anywhere or accessible to the developer.

Sharing and copying

When you share your map, the app draws the picture on your iPhone and hands it to iOS. It goes only where you send it. Copy flags puts only the flag emoji on the clipboard. The optional Instagram Story button puts the story picture on the clipboard for five minutes, for Instagram to pick up, and then opens Instagram.

Ratings and email

Now and then the app may ask you to rate it, using Apple's standard rating prompt; Apple handles any rating you give. Rate Atlas opens the App Store, and Email the developer opens a new message in your mail app with the app and iOS version filled in. The app itself sends nothing: an email reaches the developer only if you send it.

Analytics, advertising, and tracking

The app contains no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. It does not use the Advertising Identifier (IDFA), does not include any analytics or advertising SDKs, and does not follow you across other apps or websites.

Data retention and deletion

Because all data stays on your device, you are always in control of it:

  • Hide a photo or a trip in the app to stop counting it; Photo sources › Show all again brings it back.
  • Reset manual changes from the app's menu.
  • Deleting the app from your iPhone permanently removes everything it saved. Your photos stay in your library.

The developer holds no copy of your data and so cannot access, restore, or delete it for you.

Children's privacy

Atlas does not collect any personal information from anyone, including children.
